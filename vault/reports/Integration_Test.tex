\section*{Problem 1: Integral of $e^x \cos(x)$}
To evaluate the integral $\int e^x \cos(x) dx$, we will use integration by parts, which states $\int u \, dv = uv - \int v \, du$. We will apply this method twice.

Let $I = \int e^x \cos(x) dx$.

For the first application of integration by parts, let:
$u = \cos(x) \implies du = -\sin(x) dx$
$dv = e^x dx \implies v = e^x$

Applying the integration by parts formula:
\[ I = e^x \cos(x) - \int e^x (-\sin(x)) dx \]
\[ I = e^x \cos(x) + \int e^x \sin(x) dx \]

Now, we need to evaluate the integral $\int e^x \sin(x) dx$. Let's call this $J$. We apply integration by parts again to $J$.
For this second application, let:
$u = \sin(x) \implies du = \cos(x) dx$
$dv = e^x dx \implies v = e^x$

Applying the integration by parts formula to $J$:
\[ J = e^x \sin(x) - \int e^x \cos(x) dx \]
Notice that the integral on the right side, $\int e^x \cos(x) dx$, is our original integral $I$.
So, we can write $J$ in terms of $I$:
\[ J = e^x \sin(x) - I \]

Now substitute this expression for $J$ back into the equation for $I$:
\[ I = e^x \cos(x) + (e^x \sin(x) - I) \]
\[ I = e^x \cos(x) + e^x \sin(x) - I \]

Now, we solve this equation for $I$:
\[ I + I = e^x \cos(x) + e^x \sin(x) \]
\[ 2I = e^x (\cos(x) + \sin(x)) \]
\[ I = \frac{e^x}{2} (\cos(x) + \sin(x)) \]

Finally, we add the constant of integration $C$:
\[ I = \frac{e^x}{2} (\cos(x) + \sin(x)) + C \]

The final answer is $\boxed{\frac{e^x}{2}(\cos(x) + \sin(x)) + C}$.
